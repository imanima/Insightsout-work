Methodology

How we do research

InsightsOut studies the human side of AI-driven change from inside the room: what founders, leaders, and professionals share in optional workshop registration responses. This page is the standing description of how that works, and where it stops.

Where the data comes from

The first edition draws on optional registration responses from nine event waves, March to July 2026. The dataset records 444 registrations and 363 unique people. These totals are not the response count for every question, and registrations do not establish attendance.

What we ask

Questions varied across the March to July events. They covered topics such as the change people were navigating, the hardest part, emotions, role, and desired support. Each finding should be read with its own question, sample, and comparison group.

A five-question core was proposed for subsequent events: role, change, challenge, emotions, and desired support. This first edition does not establish that every later event used it. Post-event measures are a next research step, not evidence of outcomes in this dataset.

How we code it

The first edition groups responses into themes including decision uncertainty, overwhelm, role and identity, relationships, and peer support. Free-text analyses include keyword coding; questions and answer options differ across some events. Comparisons on the same form are distinguished from comparisons mapped across forms.

The dataset distinguishes registrations from unique people. That does not by itself establish how repeat responses were treated in every analysis. Response rates, missing-data rules, and independent coding agreement are not documented here; readers should not assume they have been established.

How we cross-check it

External research provides context for interpreting the findings. A similar pattern in another study does not independently validate this sample, establish causation, or make different measures directly comparable. References should be read alongside the observation and its limits.

What we will and won't publish

  • Aggregates and anonymized quotes only. No names, companies, or identifying situations. Nothing from coaching sessions or client work ever enters the research dataset.
  • A percentage appears only when the segment has at least 20 people, and a headline claim only at 50 or more. Smaller signals are labeled directional.
  • Refuted hypotheses are published, not deleted.

Limits

These are self-selected registrants, concentrated in the San Francisco and startup ecosystem. Answers describe needs and intentions before an event, not coaching or workshop outcomes. They cannot establish workforce-wide rates or prove what caused a feeling or behavior.

Questionnaires varied by event. Role was not captured at two waves, and some subgroup analyses are small. Compare the denominator for each finding rather than using the full 363-person dataset as its sample. Differences between groups may reflect who answered, wording, or other unmeasured factors.

Living papers and versions

The current papers use the August 15, 2026 findings snapshot for the March to July dataset. Their statistics are pinned to that snapshot so a later dataset update cannot silently change this edition.

The web papers include editorial corrections dated November 2, 2026. Original v1.0 PDFs remain labeled as archived editions and do not include those corrections. When citing the web text, include the correction date. Future data updates require a new identified edition and a change log.

Cite or reuse

Cite as: Imani, N. (year). Title (version). InsightsOut. URL. Reuse of aggregate figures with attribution is welcome. For the underlying dataset or collaboration, get in touch.
