The question

What should stay human?

AI can play more and more of the instruments. It can’t decide what the music is for. That part is yours. This is the question underneath everything InsightsOut does, and this page is where it lives.

Why this question

Capability is not permission.

An AI system may be able to produce an answer, a recommendation, even a decision. That doesn’t settle whether it should, who looks at it afterward, or who’s accountable if it’s wrong. Most teams haven’t asked. They find the line after something fails, instead of drawing it on purpose.

The line between human and agent is drawn, not found. What stays human, who decides, and who answers when it goes wrong. Those are leadership questions before they are technical ones, and they belong to the people doing the work.

Everyone’s answer is different. This isn’t a debate about whether AI is good or bad. It’s a practice: look honestly at what’s changing, remember what has carried people through change before, and choose.

How to decide

Four questions for a real decision.

If the honest answers are nobody, yes, yes, and no, the work stays human for now. Not because the tool is bad. Because nobody is standing behind it.

  1. Accountability. Who answers for the outcome if it’s wrong?
  2. Human impact. Could this affect someone’s opportunity, dignity, livelihood, or access?
  3. Judgment. Does the work depend on context, values, trust, or understanding a relationship?
  4. Voice. Can the people affected understand, question, or push back on what happens?

Then sort the work

Four positions.

For each part of a real workflow, pick one. Then test it with real work, notice where it fails, and redraw it. The boundary is a practice, not a document.

  1. Human-owned. A person does the work and owns the decision.
  2. AI-supported. AI helps with options or analysis. A person decides.
  3. AI-executed, human-reviewed. The AI does a bounded task and a named person checks it.
  4. Not delegated. It stays human because the relationship or the consequence requires it.
