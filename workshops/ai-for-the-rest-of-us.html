AI for the Rest of Us

An honest conversation for people who are curious about AI and a little afraid of it.

Facilitated by Nima Imani · Facilitated conversation · Frontier Tower, San Francisco · Free

← All community workshops

No demos, no jargon, and nobody makes you open a laptop. Just an honest conversation about what this shift means for your work.

What we'll do

  1. Talk about the change itself. What it feels like to work through a shift this fast.
  2. Get honest about ethics. Where using AI feels right to you, and where it doesn't. There's no company line here. People draw different lines.
  3. Look at your actual work. Sort the tasks in your week: which parts are machine work you'd hand over, and which parts are yours to keep. The care, the judgment, the relationships.

What you'll leave with

The experience of saying the uneasy part out loud. Your own ethical line. A first sort of your tasks. And people from the room to navigate it with.

Who this is for

Small business owners, coaches, consultants, farmers, makers, and independent professionals. No technical background needed.

What to bring

Yourself, and a sense of your working week.

Your facilitator

Nima Imani is an ICF-certified, ontologically trained coach with more than ten years in software, data and AI, including EY and Neo4j. He runs the weekly founder coaching group at Frontier Tower in San Francisco and founded InsightsOut.

About InsightsOut

InsightsOut (insightsout.work) is a San Francisco company founded by Nima Imani that works on the human side of AI change and leadership. It works with organizations and with individuals. For organizations, it offers leadership coaching and alignment for AI change, prepares managers and changing roles, and supports the people side of transformation. For individuals and small business owners, it runs community workshops and AI transformation consulting. For founders, it offers coaching on co-founder and team conflict, decision uncertainty, and the emotions that come with building. Underneath everything sits one purpose: helping people protect and strengthen their human agency and leadership during rapid change, backed by research built from what people share in these rooms.
