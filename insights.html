How we do research

Small questions. Careful claims.

Full method, limits, and what we won't publish: methodology →

  1. Ask at registration. Three short questions when someone signs up for a workshop: the challenge, how AI is changing their work, what they want to leave with.
  2. Code the answers. Free text is themed by hand with a conservative mapping across events; role and segment are kept where captured.
  3. Cross-check. Every claim is read against published research and marked as consistent, extending, or in tension.
  4. Publish with a version. Method, limits, changelog, and a versioned PDF — updated as new rooms land. Individual answers are never published.

Purpose and consent

Why we do this, and what we won’t do.

The research question underneath all of it: how do people keep agency, judgment, trust, and dignity as AI changes roles, teams, decisions, and the organization of work? The findings go back into the workshops and out as public knowledge.

  • Attending an event and taking part in research are separate. You can come without your answers being studied, and registering never adds you to the newsletter.
  • We collect the minimum needed for the session and the specific study, and publish aggregated themes only. Quotes and cases only with explicit permission.
  • Employers never receive identifiable emotional, performance, or coaching data. Coaching notes stay outside the research dataset.
  • Every finding carries its sample, dates, instrument changes, coding choices, and limits. This is registration data from a self-selected audience: it describes needs and intentions, not program outcomes.
